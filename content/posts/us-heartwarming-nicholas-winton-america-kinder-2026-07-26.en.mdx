John Cronin and his father Mark began with a practical question: what could John do after leaving school? Their story offers a warm example of building work around a person's interests rather than starting with assumptions about their limits.

### Looking Ahead to Life After School

In the fall of 2016, John, who has Down syndrome, was entering his final school year on Long Island, New York. According to the company's own account, he was considering his options for life after school and proposed going into business with his father.

They explored different ideas before choosing colorful socks, something John had long enjoyed. His own interests could become a starting point for choosing products and sharing them with customers.

### A Personal Touch in the First Orders

The father-son team prepared their online store, **John’s Crazy Socks**, and accepted their first orders on **December 9, 2016**. Many early customers were local. John and Mark made home deliveries with socks, candy and a thank-you note in the packages.

Customers shared photographs and their experiences, helping spread the word. The company's origin story describes those personal encounters as part of the early response to the business.

### Creating Opportunities to Contribute

The significance of the company goes beyond selling a product. John and Mark have also used their experience to highlight what people with disabilities can contribute when given an opportunity. Carleton University's 2019 account of their visit discusses inclusive entrepreneurship and employment as central themes.

There is an invitation in this story for families, coworkers and communities: begin by taking a person's interests seriously. A useful role can grow from listening, experimenting and working together. That is an editorial reflection on the story, rather than a quotation from either founder.
