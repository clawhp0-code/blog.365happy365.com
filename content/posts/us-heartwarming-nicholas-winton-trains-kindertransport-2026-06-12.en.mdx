# "That Horse Is Running for Cody" — The Story of Cody Dorman and Cody's Wish

## A Boy Who Couldn't Walk, a Foal That Wouldn't Leave

Cody Dorman was born with Wolf-Hirschhorn Syndrome, an extremely rare genetic condition that affects roughly one in 50,000 births. The disorder brought severe developmental delays, seizures, muscle weakness, and an inability to speak. Doctors told his parents, Kelly and Brandy Dorman of Louisville, Kentucky, that Cody might not survive childhood. But Cody defied every prediction, living with a radiant smile that touched everyone around him.

In 2018, through a collaboration between the Make-A-Wish Foundation and Gainesway Farm, a prestigious thoroughbred nursery in Lexington, Kentucky, Cody got the chance to visit a horse farm. It was supposed to be a simple outing — a boy in a wheelchair meeting some beautiful animals. It became something no one could have predicted.

## The Moment Everything Changed

As Cody was wheeled through the barn, most horses paid little attention to the small visitor. But one newborn foal broke from the pattern. The young horse walked directly to Cody's wheelchair, lowered its head, and gently nuzzled the boy. Then Cody had a seizure — a common and frightening occurrence with his condition. Instead of startling and pulling away, the foal pressed closer, resting its muzzle softly against Cody as if to comfort him.

When the seizure passed, Cody smiled. The farm staff wept. Antony Beck, principal of Gainesway Farm, made a decision on the spot: the foal would be named **Cody's Wish**.

## A Name That Carried Weight

As Cody's Wish grew into a powerful thoroughbred, trained by the legendary Bill Mott, the Dorman family followed every race. Cody couldn't speak, but his body language told the story — he would light up with joy whenever his horse ran.

On November 5, 2022, at Keeneland Race Course in Lexington, Kentucky, Cody's Wish entered the Breeders' Cup Dirt Mile, one of the most prestigious races in American thoroughbred racing. With Cody and his family watching from the rail, the horse surged through the field and **crossed the finish line first**.

But it was the winner's circle that broke the internet. Cody's Wish walked over to Cody's wheelchair and lowered his head, nuzzling the boy just as he had done as a foal four years earlier. Cody beamed. Tens of thousands of spectators in the stands cried openly. The video went viral, viewed millions of times around the world, and the story was featured on ESPN, NBC, and countless other outlets.

Cody's Wish was named the 2022 Eclipse Award winner for Champion Male Sprinter, but for most people, the real award was that moment with Cody.

## The Night Before the Last Race

In 2023, Cody's Wish was again entered in the Breeders' Cup Dirt Mile, this time held at Santa Anita Park in Arcadia, California. The Dorman family had planned to be there, but Cody's health had been declining steadily.

On the night of November 3, 2023 — **the evening before the race** — Cody Dorman passed away. He was 22 years old.

Kelly Dorman made the agonizing decision to let the horse run. "Cody would have wanted it," he said.

## One Last Run

On November 4, 2023, as the Santa Anita crowd learned of Cody's passing, a hush fell over the grandstands. Jockey Junior Alvarado mounted Cody's Wish with tears in his eyes. Trainer Bill Mott could barely speak during pre-race interviews.

The gates opened. Cody's Wish broke cleanly, settled into stride, and in the final stretch, powered ahead of the field. He crossed the finish line **first** — a repeat Breeders' Cup champion. Back-to-back Dirt Mile victories.

Alvarado pointed to the sky. Mott buried his face in his hands. Kelly Dorman, watching from afar on the worst and most miraculous day of his life, told reporters:

> **"Cody left us last night. And today, Cody's Wish ran for him one last time. I believe Cody was riding that horse."**

The racing world — an industry not typically associated with sentimentality — collectively broke down. Hardened jockeys, trainers, and track officials were seen weeping openly. The Breeders' Cup broadcast became one of the most emotionally powerful moments in the history of American sports television.

## The Legacy of a Wish

Cody Dorman never spoke a word in his life. He never walked a step on his own. And yet, through one horse, he told one of the most powerful stories in modern American sports.

Cody's Wish retired after the 2023 season with a career record of seven wins from twelve starts and earnings of approximately $4 million. But no dollar figure can capture what this horse meant — not to the Dorman family, not to the racing community, and not to the millions of people around the world who watched a horse lower its head to a boy in a wheelchair and saw, in that simple gesture, everything that is good about the bond between humans and animals.

The Make-A-Wish Foundation, which facilitated the original farm visit, called it one of the most remarkable wish stories in the organization's history. The Dorman family has since become advocates for Wolf-Hirschhorn Syndrome awareness, carrying Cody's legacy forward.

In a quiet barn in Kentucky in 2018, a foal chose a boy. And in doing so, he gave the world a reminder: love doesn't require words. Sometimes, it just requires showing up — and running as fast as you can for the one who needs you most.

Cody, your wish came true.
