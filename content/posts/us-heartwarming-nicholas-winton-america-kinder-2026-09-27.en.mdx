# "We Won't Stand Down" — The Three Strangers Who Shielded Two Girls from Hate on a Portland Train

## An Ordinary Friday Evening

May 26, 2017. Portland, Oregon. The Friday afternoon MAX Light Rail was filled with commuters heading home for the long Memorial Day weekend. Among the passengers were two teenage girls — one of them, sixteen-year-old Destinee Mangum, sat beside her friend who wore a hijab. It was a quiet evening before Ramadan.

Then a man approached them.

Jeremy Christian, 35, began unleashing a torrent of anti-Muslim, racist slurs at the two girls. His voice grew louder. His threats grew more violent. The girls froze. Most passengers looked away or moved to the far end of the car.

But three men stood up.

## Three Strangers, One Decision

**Ricky Best**, 53, was a retired U.S. Army veteran of 23 years who worked as a technician for the City of Portland. He was a father of four, a man whose family described him as someone who "never walked away from someone who needed help."

**Taliesin Myrddin Namkai-Meche**, 23, had just graduated from Reed College with a degree in economics. His friends called him "the kindest person they had ever known." He was planning to start a career in public service.

**Micah Fletcher**, 21, was a Portland State University student studying creative writing and poetry. He was tall, lanky, and by his own admission, "not a fighter — just someone who couldn't sit still when something was wrong."

None of them knew each other. But in the same moment, they made the same choice. They stepped between Jeremy Christian and the two girls and told him to stop.

Christian responded with a knife.

## The Attack

In seconds, the encounter turned catastrophic. Christian slashed and stabbed all three men. Taliesin was struck in the neck and collapsed. Ricky Best suffered deep stab wounds and fell to the floor of the train car. Micah Fletcher was slashed across the neck but, by a matter of millimeters, the blade missed his carotid artery.

As the train pulled into the Hollywood Transit Center station, Micah staggered off the car, blood pouring from his neck, calling for help. Bystanders rushed to aid the victims. Emergency services arrived within minutes.

Ricky Best died at the scene. Taliesin Myrddin Namkai-Meche was rushed to the hospital but died that evening. Two lives — a father who had served his country for decades and a young man just beginning his — were gone.

The two girls escaped physically unharmed.

## "Tell Everyone on This Train I Love Them"

Witnesses later recounted that as Taliesin lay dying on the train floor, he spoke his final words: **"Tell everyone on this train I love them."**

His mother, Anda French, shared this in the days that followed. "My son died doing exactly what he would have wanted to do," she said through tears. "He stood up for people he didn't know because he believed every human being deserves to be safe."

Ricky Best's son Erik remembered his father simply: "Dad was a soldier his whole life. Even after the uniform came off, he was still a soldier. He didn't know how to stand down."

## Micah Fletcher: The Survivor's Burden and Mission

Micah Fletcher survived with a deep wound across his neck that required extensive surgery. In the weeks and months that followed, he grappled with survivor's guilt — the unbearable weight of being the one who lived.

At the trial in 2020, Micah testified with quiet intensity. "I'm not a hero," he told the court. "Rick and Taliesin are the heroes. They stood first. I only followed."

After his recovery, Micah dedicated himself to speaking out against hate crimes. He visited schools, community centers, and public forums across Oregon and beyond, sharing the story not to celebrate violence but to remind people of what courage looks like in its most raw and costly form.

As a poet, he processed his grief through verse. One of his most quoted lines reads: *"We were strangers. But in that moment, we were family."*

## A City Mourns, a Nation Reflects

Portland was shaken to its core. The Hollywood Transit Center station became a memorial overnight — thousands of flowers, handwritten letters, candles, and photographs blanketed the platform. Thousands gathered for a candlelight vigil. Signs reading **"We Won't Stand Down"** appeared throughout the city.

The Oregon state legislature honored Best and Namkai-Meche, and Portland awarded its highest civilian commendation to all three men. Nationally, the story sparked renewed conversations about hate crimes, Islamophobia, and the moral responsibility of bystanders.

## Justice

In February 2020, Jeremy Christian was found guilty of two counts of first-degree murder, one count of attempted murder, and other charges. He was sentenced to two consecutive life sentences without the possibility of parole.

After the verdict, Anda French — Taliesin's mother — stood outside the courthouse and spoke to reporters. Her words carried no bitterness, only a quiet, unbreakable resolve:

> "This verdict doesn't bring my son back. But what my son showed the world will last forever. Hate screams loudly, but love acts louder."

## The Legacy

The story of the Portland train heroes endures not because of the horror of the attack, but because of the choice three ordinary men made in an extraordinary moment. They did not know the girls they protected. They had no weapons, no training for this scenario, no time to calculate the risk. They simply stood up.

Ricky Best's family established a memorial fund for veterans' families. Taliesin's loved ones created a scholarship in his name at Reed College, awarded to students committed to social justice. Micah Fletcher continues to write, speak, and advocate, carrying the memory of two men he met for only a few seconds but will remember for the rest of his life.

In a world that often asks, "Why bother? Why risk yourself for a stranger?" — Rick Best, Taliesin Myrddin Namkai-Meche, and Micah Fletcher answered with their bodies, their blood, and their lives.

**They would not stand down.**
