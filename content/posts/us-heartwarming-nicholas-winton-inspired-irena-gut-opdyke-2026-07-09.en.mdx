# "That Horse Ran for Cody" — The Extraordinary Bond Between a Boy and a Racehorse

## A Boy Who Was Never Supposed to Make It

In 2006, **Cody Dorman** was born in Georgetown, Kentucky, with a rare genetic condition called **Wolf-Hirschhorn Syndrome**. Caused by a deletion on the short arm of chromosome 4, the disorder brings severe developmental delays, intellectual disabilities, heart defects, and diminished muscle tone. Doctors warned the Dorman family that Cody might not survive long.

But **Kelly and Tony Dorman** refused to let a prognosis define their son's life. They were determined to fill Cody's days with as much wonder as possible. Despite being confined to a wheelchair and unable to speak in full sentences, Cody radiated joy. His smile was infectious, and his eyes lit up at one thing above all else: **horses**.

## The Foal That Chose Him

Growing up in the heart of Kentucky's Bluegrass Country, Cody had access to some of the finest horse farms in the world. His family made regular visits to local thoroughbred operations, and every time Cody was near horses, he came alive — laughing, reaching out, trembling with excitement.

In 2018, the Dorman family visited **Gainesway Farm**, a storied breeding operation in Lexington, Kentucky. That day, a newborn dark bay foal did something unusual. While thoroughbred foals are typically skittish around strangers, this one walked directly up to Cody's wheelchair, nuzzled against the boy's knees, and gently laid its head in Cody's lap.

The sight moved everyone present. The farm's team decided this foal's life and Cody's were intertwined. As the colt grew and was eventually named **Rietbrock**, the Dorman family became his honorary cheering section. Every time the horse ran, Cody was there — in person when possible, on a screen when not.

## A Horse That Could Run Like the Wind

Rietbrock developed into a formidable racehorse. Trained by **Chad Brown**, one of America's premier trainers, and owned by **Klaravich Stables**, the colt showed promise on the turf from his earliest starts. By the fall of 2023, Rietbrock had earned a spot in one of the most prestigious races in American horse racing: the **Breeders' Cup Juvenile Turf** at Santa Anita Park in Arcadia, California.

For Cody, now 17 years old, the prospect of "his" horse running on the biggest stage was the ultimate dream. His family planned to watch together, as they always did.

## The Day Before

On **November 2, 2023**, the day before the Breeders' Cup Juvenile Turf, Cody Dorman passed away. After 17 years of defying medical expectations, his body could fight no longer. The Dorman family was devastated.

News of Cody's passing reached the racing community quickly. Chad Brown, jockey **Flavien Prat**, and the team at Klaravich Stables were heartbroken. But there was no question about what would happen the next day.

**They would run — for Cody.**

## The Race

On **November 3, 2023**, Rietbrock loaded into the starting gate for the Breeders' Cup Juvenile Turf. The field was deep with talented two-year-olds from around the world. Flavien Prat settled the colt in a stalking position through the early fractions, saving ground along the rail.

Turning for home, Rietbrock found another gear. As the field straightened in the stretch, the dark bay colt surged forward, weaving between horses, accelerating with breathtaking power. He crossed the finish line **first**.

**Breeders' Cup Juvenile Turf Champion.**

Prat pointed skyward as he galloped out. Tears flowed in the winner's circle. Tony Dorman, despite the weight of his grief, stood in the California sunshine and wept.

> "Cody was riding that horse. I know he was. My boy ran that race from heaven."

## A Story That Touched Millions

The story of Cody and Rietbrock swept across American media. ESPN, NBC, CNN, and countless outlets carried the tale. Social media was flooded with tributes. People who had never watched a horse race in their lives found themselves in tears.

What resonated was something deeper than sport. It was the image of a boy who could never walk, whose body was fragile and failing, but whose spirit found freedom through a horse that once, as a foal, had chosen him. It was a reminder that connection transcends physical limitation — that love does not need legs to run.

## Legacy

The Dorman family channeled their grief into purpose. They continued to raise awareness for **Wolf-Hirschhorn Syndrome** through community events and social media. A portion of Rietbrock's earnings was directed toward charities supporting children with rare diseases.

Rietbrock continued racing in 2024, and every time he ran, the racing world remembered the boy in the wheelchair whose name was whispered in the wind of the homestretch.

Kelly Dorman later said in an interview:

> "When Rietbrock ran, Cody wasn't disabled. He was free. He was flying."
