# "My Son's Heart Is Still Beating" — The Story of Nicholas Green and the Father Who Gave Life to a Nation

## A Dream Vacation Turns to Nightmare

In late September 1994, **Reg Green**, a British-born American journalist living in Bodega Bay, California, set out on a family holiday to Italy with his wife **Maggie** and their two children — seven-year-old **Nicholas** and four-year-old **Eleanor**. Reg wanted his children to walk through the ruins of ancient Rome, to see the Mediterranean sparkle under the southern sun. Nicholas, bright-eyed and endlessly curious, was enchanted by everything he saw.

On the night of September 29, the family was driving south on the **A3 motorway** between Salerno and Reggio Calabria in the region of Calabria. A car pulled alongside them. Without warning, gunshots erupted. The attackers — later identified as petty criminals who mistook the family's rental car for a jeweler's vehicle — fired into the car. Reg floored the accelerator and sped away, but when the terror subsided, he discovered the unthinkable: Nicholas, asleep in the back seat, had been struck in the head by a bullet.

## Two Days of Hope, Then Silence

Nicholas was rushed to a hospital in Messina, Sicily, where doctors fought to save him. For two agonizing days, Reg and Maggie stood vigil. On October 1, 1994, physicians pronounced Nicholas brain-dead. His small heart was still beating, but their boy would never open his eyes again.

The Greens were thousands of miles from home, in the very country where strangers had murdered their child. Every instinct of grief might have driven them toward rage, toward bitter withdrawal. Instead, Reg and Maggie made a decision that stunned the world.

**They chose to donate Nicholas's organs to the Italian people.**

## The Gift of Seven Lives

Nicholas's organs and corneas were transplanted into **seven Italian recipients**:

- His **heart** went to **Andrea Mongiardo**, a 15-year-old boy suffering from a severe cardiac condition.
- His **liver** saved a 19-year-old Sicilian.
- One **kidney** went to a pediatric nurse; the other to a man who had been on dialysis.
- His two **corneas** restored sight to two patients.
- His **pancreatic islet cells** were transplanted to a diabetic patient.

Every transplant was successful. Seven people who had been facing death or disability were given new life by a seven-year-old American boy they had never met.

## "I Can Hear Him"

Years later, Reg visited Andrea, now a healthy young man. A doctor placed a stethoscope against Andrea's chest and handed the earpieces to Reg. For the first time since that terrible night on the highway, Reg heard his son's heartbeat.

> *"It was the most extraordinary moment of my life,"* Reg said. *"Nicholas was gone, but there he was — still beating, still alive inside someone else."*

## The Nicholas Effect

At the time of Nicholas's death, Italy had one of the **lowest organ donation rates in Europe**. Deep cultural and religious reservations meant that families overwhelmingly refused donation. Waiting lists were long, and patients routinely died waiting.

Nicholas's story broke through every barrier. The image of American parents — victims of Italian violence — offering their child's body as a gift to the very country that had taken him was almost incomprehensible in its generosity. Italians were moved on a scale rarely seen in public life.

In the years following Nicholas's death, Italy's organ donation rate **more than tripled**. Researchers and ethicists gave this phenomenon a name: **"L'Effetto Nicholas" — The Nicholas Effect.** It became one of the most studied examples of how a single human story can reshape public policy and cultural norms around organ donation.

The Italian government awarded Reg Green the **Gold Medal of the Italian Republic**, one of the nation's highest civilian honors. Schools, parks, streets, hospital wards, and scholarships across Italy were named after Nicholas. In the town of Bodega Bay, an annual ceremony commemorates the boy whose brief life became an enduring bridge between two nations.

## A Father's Mission

Returning to California, Reg devoted the rest of his life to **organ donation advocacy**. He wrote a book, ***The Nicholas Effect: A Boy's Gift to the World***, and spoke at conferences, universities, and hospitals around the globe. He and Maggie later had twins, and Reg raised them with the memory of their older brother always present — not as a source of sorrow, but as a reminder of what one life can mean.

Reg has said:

> *"Nicholas only lived seven years. But in those seven years, and in what came after, he saved more lives than most of us will in a full lifetime. Children can change the world — while they're alive, and even after they're gone."*

## Thirty Years Later

As of 2024, three decades have passed since that night on the Italian highway. Andrea Mongiardo — the boy who received Nicholas's heart — grew up, married, and started a family of his own. He tells his children about the American boy whose heart beats inside his chest.

Every year, Italian communities hold remembrance events for Nicholas. The donation rates that surged in the wake of his story have remained high, saving thousands of lives that might otherwise have been lost.

And somewhere in southern Italy, a heart that once belonged to a curious, bright-eyed boy from California continues to beat — steady, strong, and full of a father's love that chose grace over grief, life over death, and giving over keeping.

## Why This Story Matters

The Nicholas Green story is more than an organ donation case study. It is a testament to the radical power of generosity in the face of the worst that humanity can do. Reg and Maggie Green did not merely forgive; they transformed their loss into a mechanism of salvation that has rippled outward for thirty years and counting.

In a world that often responds to violence with more violence, to loss with bitterness, the Greens remind us that another path exists — one where a seven-year-old boy's final gift can change the moral landscape of an entire nation.

Nicholas Green lived seven years. His heart has been beating for thirty more. And the lives he saved have gone on to create families, pursue dreams, and carry forward a legacy that proves, beyond any doubt, that love is stronger than death.
