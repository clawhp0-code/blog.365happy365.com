## A Cold Night Near Times Square

November 14, 2012. The temperature in New York City had dropped near freezing, and the streets around Times Square were filled with the usual mix of tourists, theatergoers, and holiday shoppers. Amid the neon glow and the crowds, one figure sat almost invisible on the sidewalk — a man with no shoes, no socks, and feet covered in blisters and the early signs of frostbite.

**Officer Lawrence "Larry" DePrimo**, a 25-year-old patrolman from Holbrook, Long Island, was working a counter-terrorism detail in the area that night. He had graduated from the police academy not long before and was still relatively new to the force. Nothing about his assignment that evening called for what he was about to do.

## "I Had to Do Something"

As DePrimo walked his beat near the corner of 42nd Street and Seventh Avenue, he noticed the barefoot man sitting on the pavement. He stopped. He looked at the man's feet — raw, swollen, pressed against the freezing concrete — and something inside him simply would not let him walk away.

Without hesitation, DePrimo walked into a nearby Skechers shoe store. He asked a store employee for the warmest boots they had. He pulled out his own wallet and paid **$75** — not an insignificant sum on a rookie officer's salary — for a pair of all-weather boots and a pair of thermal socks.

He returned to the man, knelt down on one knee on the cold sidewalk, and carefully slipped the socks and boots onto the man's feet.

He didn't call for a camera crew. He didn't post about it on social media. He simply did it and moved on with his patrol.

## The Photograph That Changed Everything

What DePrimo didn't know was that someone was watching. **Jennifer Foster**, a tourist visiting from Arizona who worked in the communications field, had witnessed the entire scene from across the street. Moved to tears, she pulled out her phone and snapped a photograph: the young officer, in full uniform with his NYPD vest, kneeling on the ground, gently putting boots on the barefoot stranger.

Foster later emailed the photo to the NYPD's official Facebook page with a message:

> *"I just wanted to share with you what I witnessed tonight. An NYPD officer was kneeling down, putting boots on a barefoot homeless man. This is the most beautiful thing I have ever seen in New York City."*

On November 27, 2012, the NYPD posted the image. Within hours, it went viral. Within days, the post had received **more than 1.6 million likes** and hundreds of thousands of shares, making it one of the most-liked Facebook posts in history at that time. Major news outlets around the world — CNN, NBC, ABC, the BBC, and dozens more — picked up the story.

## A Humble Response

Suddenly thrust into the global spotlight, DePrimo remained remarkably humble. In interviews, he said:

> *"I didn't think anything of it. I just saw a man who needed help. His feet were blistered, and it was freezing out. I don't think there's anyone who wouldn't have done the same thing."*

He also shared a detail that resonated deeply with millions:

> *"When I put those boots on him, the look on his face — that smile — was worth more than a million dollars to me."*

DePrimo revealed that he had kept the receipt from the boots in his wallet. Not because of the cost, but because he wanted to **remember the moment** and the feeling it gave him.

## The Larger Reality

The story took a more complex turn when journalists tracked down the barefoot man. His name was **Jeffrey Hillman**, and he was a military veteran who had struggled with homelessness for years despite having access to certain services. When reporters found him days later, he was once again barefoot. Hillman explained that he had hidden the boots because on the streets, having a nice pair of shoes made you a target for theft.

This revelation didn't diminish DePrimo's act of kindness. Instead, it illuminated the **deeper, systemic challenges** of homelessness in America — the fact that a single pair of boots, however lovingly given, cannot solve a crisis that requires sustained support, mental health services, housing, and community commitment. The conversation that DePrimo's act sparked was, in many ways, as valuable as the act itself.

## A Ripple Effect

The impact of that one photograph extended far beyond a viral moment:

- **The NYPD** officially commended DePrimo, and he was promoted to detective in the years that followed.
- **Donations surged** to homeless shelters and organizations across New York City and beyond, as people were inspired to give.
- The story became a touchstone for the **"Random Acts of Kindness"** movement, inspiring countless people to perform small, meaningful gestures for strangers.
- Schools across the country used the story in character-education programs, teaching children that heroism doesn't always require extraordinary circumstances — sometimes it just requires **paying attention**.

DePrimo continued to serve quietly and faithfully with the NYPD, never seeking to capitalize on his fame. He remained the same modest young man from Long Island who simply couldn't walk past someone in pain.

## What $75 Can Buy

Larry DePrimo spent $75 that night. In return, he gave the world something priceless: a reminder that compassion is not a grand, sweeping gesture reserved for extraordinary people. It is a choice available to every single one of us, every single day.

It is the choice to stop walking. To kneel down. To meet someone at eye level. To say, without words: *I see you. You matter.*

On a freezing November night in New York City, a young police officer knelt on a cold sidewalk and put boots on a stranger's feet. And in that small, quiet act, he reminded millions of people around the world what it means to be human.

> *"That smile was worth more than a million dollars."*
> — Officer Larry DePrimo
