## A Life Full of Ordinary Beauty

Kara Tippetts (1976–2015) was, by most outward measures, a wonderfully ordinary woman. She and her husband Jason planted a small church community in Colorado Springs, Colorado, and together they were raising four children. Kara was known in her neighborhood for her open door, her easy laughter, and her willingness to sit with anyone going through a hard season. Friends called her "warmth on two legs."

In 2012, at the age of thirty-six, Kara was diagnosed with breast cancer. She endured surgery and chemotherapy, and for a time, hope flickered brightly. But by late 2013, the cancer had metastasized — to her bones, her brain, her liver. Stage four. The doctors told her gently that her remaining time was limited.

## Choosing to Count Grace

Many people, upon hearing such a prognosis, would understandably retreat into fear or despair. Kara made a different choice. She decided to record the small beauties of every remaining day.

She opened a blog called **"Mundane Faithfulness"** — a name that captured everything she believed. Faithfulness wasn't found in grand gestures. It was found in the mundane: in braiding a daughter's hair, in laughing at a son's joke, in watching snow fall through a window while pain pulsed through her body.

Her writing was disarmingly honest. She didn't pretend the suffering wasn't real. She wrote about the terror of leaving her children, the humiliation of losing her hair, the nights when the pain was so severe she couldn't hold her babies. But woven through every post was an insistence that love was still present, that beauty had not abandoned her.

> *"Today my hair fell out in clumps again. The kids wanted to make art with it. We cried and laughed at the same time."*

> *"The pain was so bad I couldn't hug my children. My four-year-old held just one of my fingers and said, 'Mama, this much hug is enough.'"*

The blog went viral. Within months, hundreds of thousands of readers from around the world were following Kara's journey — not just cancer patients, but people battling depression, divorce, grief, and every shade of human heartbreak. Her words became a lifeline.

## A Book, a Bestseller, and a Letter to Brittany

In 2014, Kara published **"The Hardest Peace: Expecting Grace in the Midst of Life's Hard."** The book became a bestseller almost immediately. In graceful, unflinching prose, she laid bare what it meant to face death while fiercely loving life.

That same year, another young woman's story captivated America. Brittany Maynard, twenty-nine years old and diagnosed with terminal brain cancer, publicly announced her decision to end her own life through Oregon's Death with Dignity Act. The nation erupted in debate.

Kara wrote Brittany an open letter. It was not a lecture. It was not a condemnation. It was one dying woman reaching out to another with gentleness:

> *"Brittany, I know your fear. I share it. But I am asking you — please don't pick your releasing day. There is still so much surprising love waiting for you in the days you haven't lived yet."*

The letter was read by millions. CNN, NBC, and *The Washington Post* covered Kara's story. The conversation shifted. It was no longer just about politics or policy — it became about what it means to find purpose inside suffering, about the stubborn, radical act of choosing to stay present even when staying is agonizing.

## Letters to Her Children

As 2015 began, Kara's body was failing. She could no longer leave her bed. But she kept writing — not for the world this time, but for the four small people who would have to grow up without her.

To her oldest son, she wrote: *"I wanted to watch you become a brave man. Even though I won't see it, I already know — you will be."*

To her second daughter: *"Your laugh is the sound Mama will remember even in heaven."*

She wrote to each child individually, by hand, pouring a lifetime of unspoken motherhood into pages they could hold for decades to come.

To Jason, she said simply: *"Tell them I didn't leave because I was afraid. Tell them I left still loving."*

On **March 22, 2015**, surrounded by her family, Kara Tippetts closed her eyes for the last time. She was thirty-nine years old.

## A Legacy That Keeps Growing

Jason kept the blog online. To this day, Mundane Faithfulness receives tens of thousands of visitors each month. Kara's words continue to reach people in hospice beds, in hospital waiting rooms, in dark apartments where someone is wondering if tomorrow is worth waking up for.

The church community in Colorado Springs launched **"Kara's Garden,"** a local outreach project that delivers meals to families with terminal diagnoses, provides childcare, and simply sits with patients — embodying the ministry of presence that Kara believed was the most powerful gift one human could give another.

Her book has been translated into twelve languages. It is quietly pressed into the hands of grieving parents, exhausted caregivers, and anyone searching for proof that beauty does not require the absence of pain.

## What Suffering Taught Her — and Us

Kara Tippetts's story resonates so deeply because she never pretended. She admitted to rage, to terror, to nights of weeping so fierce she thought she might break. She did not offer toxic positivity or hollow platitudes. What she offered was something far more durable: the testimony that even inside the worst suffering a human can endure, there are still moments of startling grace — if you are willing to look.

A child's crooked drawing. The warmth of soup made by a husband's hands. Morning sunlight through a window.

She wrote:

> *"The beauty of life doesn't come when suffering ends. It comes when you discover you can still love in the middle of it. That is beauty."*

Thirty-nine years. By any measure, it was not enough time. But Kara Tippetts filled those years so completely with love and honesty that her life continues to ripple outward, touching strangers she never met, in countries she never visited, in moments of darkness she never witnessed.

Ten years after her passing, somewhere in the world right now, someone is opening her blog for the first time. They are reading her words through tears. And they are encountering a single, luminous truth:

**"Today's grace is enough."**

And once more, they decide to live.
