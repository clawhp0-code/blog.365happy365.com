# "Instead of Birthday Gifts, Please Give Clean Water"
## The Miracle Left Behind by 9-Year-Old Rachael Beckwith

---

### A Little Girl with a Big Heart

In the spring of 2011, **Rachael Beckwith**, a quiet, compassionate girl living near Seattle, Washington, learned something in her church's Sunday school that she couldn't shake: millions of children around the world were getting sick and dying because they had no access to clean drinking water.

For most nine-year-olds, this information might linger for an afternoon and then fade. But Rachael carried it with her. As her ninth birthday approached in June 2011, she made an unusual request to her mother, **Samantha Paul Beckwith**.

"Mom, I don't want birthday presents this year. I want people to donate to charity: water instead."

She opened a fundraising page on the website of **charity: water**, a nonprofit organization founded by **Scott Harrison** that builds wells and water systems in developing nations. Rachael set her goal at **$300** — enough to help fund a single clean water project in a community that had none.

---

### Falling Short — and a Promise for Next Year

Rachael's birthday came and went. Family members and friends contributed what they could. The total reached **$220** — a generous sum by any measure, but $80 short of her goal. Rachael was a little disappointed but far from defeated.

"It's okay, Mom," she said. "I'll do it again next year. Next time I'll make it to $300."

She never got that chance.

---

### July 20, 2011

On July 20, 2011, Rachael was riding in a vehicle on Interstate 90 near Seattle when it became caught in a multi-car pileup. She was rushed to Harborview Medical Center with severe injuries. Doctors fought for three days, but Rachael never regained consciousness.

On **July 23, 2011**, her family made the agonizing decision to remove life support. Rachael Beckwith was gone. She was nine years old.

---

### The World Discovers Rachael's Wish

As Samantha prepared to bury her daughter, she remembered the charity: water campaign page — still frozen at $220. In her grief, she shared Rachael's story online, gently asking if anyone might help her daughter's modest dream come true.

What happened next stunned everyone.

Within hours, donations began pouring in. The $300 goal was surpassed in a single day. Then the total hit $1,000. Then $10,000. Then $100,000. News outlets — NBC, ABC, CNN, The Huffington Post — picked up the story of the little girl whose dying wish was clean water for strangers she would never meet.

Scott Harrison, founder of charity: water, later recalled: *"We've seen thousands of campaigns on our platform, but nothing like this. A nine-year-old girl was moving the entire world."*

---

### $1.2 Million and 37,000 Lives

By the time the campaign closed, Rachael's page had raised **$1,265,823**. With those funds, charity: water built **143 clean water projects** across Ethiopia, Uganda, Bangladesh, and other countries in desperate need. More than **37,000 people** gained access to safe drinking water for the first time in their lives — because of one child's birthday wish.

In a village in Ethiopia, a well was dedicated in Rachael's name. When photographs of children drinking clean water from that well reached Samantha, she wept.

"Rachael was so upset she didn't reach $300," Samantha said. "If she can see this from heaven, I know she's smiling the biggest smile."

---

### A Legacy That Never Runs Dry

Rachael Beckwith did not live long enough to learn long division or attend her first school dance. She never traveled to Africa to see the wells her compassion would build. She simply believed that no child should die from dirty water — and she acted on that belief with the only tool she had: a birthday.

After Rachael's passing, her mother began speaking at schools, churches, and conferences across the country, carrying a simple message: **You are never too young and never too small to change the world.**

Charity: water preserved Rachael's campaign page as a permanent testament to what one person's generosity can spark. Her story has since inspired thousands of other children to launch their own fundraising campaigns, creating a ripple effect that continues to bring clean water to communities in need.

Rachael once wrote in a school journal: *"I want to help people."* Four words. No qualifiers, no conditions, no limits on who deserved help. Just a pure, unfiltered desire to make someone else's life better.

She succeeded beyond anything she could have imagined.

---

### Remembering Rachael

**Rachael Beckwith**
January 12, 2002 – July 23, 2011

Nine years of life. Over a million dollars of love. Thirty-seven thousand people who will never thirst because a little girl in Seattle cared.

Some wells run dry. The one Rachael dug in the hearts of the world never will.
