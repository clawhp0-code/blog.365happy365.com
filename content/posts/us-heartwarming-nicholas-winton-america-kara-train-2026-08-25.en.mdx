## "Don't Worry, Mom. I Can Dance."

### The Story of Amy Purdy, Who Lost Both Legs and Danced Her Way Back to Life

---

### A Life Interrupted at Nineteen

In 1999, Amy Purdy was a nineteen-year-old living in Las Vegas, Nevada. She worked as a massage therapist during the week and spent her weekends carving through fresh powder on her snowboard. Life was full of limitless promise. She dreamed of traveling the world, riding every mountain she could find.

Then, without warning, what seemed like the flu turned into something catastrophic. Amy was rushed to the hospital, where doctors diagnosed her with **bacterial meningitis** caused by *Neisseria meningitidis*. The infection had already invaded her bloodstream, triggering septic shock. Her organs began shutting down one by one.

Her doctors gave her a **2% chance of survival**.

---

### Surviving — at an Unimaginable Cost

Against all medical odds, Amy survived. But the price was devastating. Surgeons had to amputate **both legs below the knee**. Her **spleen** was removed. Her **kidneys** failed, and she would need a transplant. For more than two months, she drifted in and out of consciousness in an ICU bed.

When she finally awoke and understood what had happened — that the legs she had used to snowboard, to dance, to simply walk across a room were gone — the despair was crushing.

> *"The hardest part wasn't losing my legs. It was losing the idea of who I thought I was supposed to be."*

Her father, William Purdy, stepped forward and donated one of his kidneys to save her life. When his kidney began functioning inside her body, Amy recognized a profound truth: her survival stood on someone else's sacrifice. She owed it to that sacrifice to truly *live*.

---

### "If I Don't Have Legs, I'll Build the Ones I Want"

During her rehabilitation, Amy was fitted with standard prosthetic legs. They were functional for walking but completely useless for the one thing she wanted most: snowboarding. Most people would have accepted that and moved on.

Amy refused.

> *"Not having legs meant I could choose how tall I wanted to be. I could design legs that could do things biological legs couldn't."*

She began collaborating with prosthetists to design custom prosthetic legs specifically engineered for snowboarding. The process was brutal — prototypes broke, she fell countless times, and the pain of impact on fresh surgical sites was excruciating. The first time she strapped into a board on her new legs, she crashed within seconds.

But the fourth time, she stood. And rode.

By 2003, Amy was competing in **USASA (United States of America Snowboard Association)** events, earning medals alongside able-bodied athletes. The snowboarding world took notice of this remarkable woman who treated her prosthetics not as limitations but as engineering opportunities.

---

### Sochi 2014: Standing on the World Stage

Amy became a leading advocate for the inclusion of **adaptive snowboarding** in the Paralympic Games. Her efforts, along with those of other adaptive athletes, paid off when the International Paralympic Committee added the sport to the program for the **2014 Sochi Winter Paralympics** — the very first time the event had been held.

Amy Purdy competed in that historic inaugural event and won the **bronze medal** in the snowboard cross.

Standing on the podium, tears streaming down her face, the full arc of her journey crystallized: fifteen years earlier, she had been given a 2% chance of simply staying alive. Now the entire world was watching her hold a Paralympic medal.

---

### Dancing on Prosthetics — *Dancing with the Stars*

In the spring of 2014, fresh off her Paralympic triumph, Amy joined Season 18 of ABC's **Dancing with the Stars**, paired with professional dancer **Derek Hough**. Skeptics wondered how a double amputee could possibly compete in a ballroom dance competition.

From her very first performance, Amy silenced every doubt. She moved with grace, power, and raw emotion. In some routines, she removed her prosthetic legs entirely and danced on her knees, transforming vulnerability into breathtaking art.

Judge Carrie Ann Inaba wept openly after one performance and told her:

> *"You are not a person with a disability. You are a person with wings."*

Amy advanced all the way to the **finale**, finishing as the **runner-up** for the season. Multiple episodes featuring her performances drew the season's highest viewer votes. She had not merely competed; she had redefined what dance could be.

---

### A Legacy Beyond the Podium

Amy's **TED Talk**, delivered in 2011, has been viewed over **seven million times**. In it, she posed a question that continues to echo:

> *"If your life were a book and you were the author, how would you want your story to go?"*

She co-founded **Adaptive Action Sports**, a nonprofit organization that introduces young people with physical disabilities to action sports like snowboarding, skateboarding, and surfing. Through the organization, thousands of disabled youth have experienced the thrill of standing on a board for the first time, and many have gone on to become competitive athletes themselves.

In 2015, she published her memoir, **_On My Own Two Feet_** — a title that is itself a declaration of defiance and hope. She has since worked as a motivational speaker, actress, model, and entrepreneur.

In 2020, Amy and her husband **Daniel Giersch** welcomed their first child, marking yet another chapter in a life defined by beginnings rather than endings.

---

### What Amy Teaches Us

Amy Purdy's story resonates so deeply because she refused to let the worst moment of her life define her. When the world handed her a list of things she could no longer do, she tore it up and wrote her own.

She lost her legs, her spleen, and her kidneys at nineteen. She gained a Paralympic medal, a global platform, and the knowledge that **limitations are not walls — they are starting lines**.

Her TED Talk ends with a challenge to every listener:

> *"Are you going to let your circumstances define you, or are you going to define your circumstances?"*

Sometimes the most devastating chapter of a life becomes the first page of its most beautiful story. Amy Purdy proved it — standing on two titanium legs, dancing before millions, carving through snow on a mountain she was never supposed to see again.

She is still writing her story. And the pages keep getting better.
