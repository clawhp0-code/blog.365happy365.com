## Prologue: The Child Who Might Never Walk

Born in January 1996 in Manchester, Connecticut, **Alexandra "Alex" Scott** was diagnosed with **neuroblastoma**, the most common form of cancer in infants, before she even turned one. Doctors gently warned her parents, Liz and Jay Scott, that even if Alex survived, she might never walk.

Two weeks later, Alex moved her leg slightly when her parents asked her to kick. By her second birthday, she was crawling, and soon after, standing with the help of leg braces. One by one, she proved the doctors wrong, relearning how to walk with sheer determination.

---

## The Sentence From a Hospital Bed

In 2000, the day after her fourth birthday, Alex underwent a major stem cell transplant. It was a grueling procedure for any child. But when it was over, what she said to her mother surprised everyone.

> *"When I get out of the hospital, I want to have a lemonade stand."*

She wanted to help other children who were sick like her. Her parents took their young daughter's wish seriously. With her older brother's help, they set up a small table in the front yard. Charging less than a dollar a cup, Alex's very first stand raised **$2,000** — every cent of which went to her hospital.

---

## A Small Stand That Grew Every Year

From then on, Alex held a lemonade stand in her front yard every year. What started with a handful of neighbors soon drew crowds as word spread. Local newspapers picked up her story, and before long, national media took notice. People didn't come just for the lemonade — they came for the courage and sincerity of the girl behind the table.

Alex never hid the fact that she was fighting cancer. Instead, she told anyone who would listen — kids and adults alike — that she wanted other sick children to get better treatment. For her, the lemonade stand wasn't a business. It was an invitation to the world.

---

## Age Eight, a Final Goodbye

Cancer continued to take its toll on Alex's body, but she never stopped setting up her stand, even while undergoing treatment. On August 1, 2004, Alex Scott passed away at the age of eight.

By the time she died, the money raised by one little girl's lemonade stand had already surpassed **$1 million**. A single sentence spoken from a hospital bed had, in just four years, moved the hearts of a million people.

---

## Parents Who Carried the Torch

In January 2005, Alex's parents, Liz and Jay, officially founded the **Alex's Lemonade Stand Foundation** in their daughter's name. What Alex had started, the foundation would now continue.

Children across the country began setting up their own lemonade stands in their front yards, following Alex's example. Schools, companies, and communities joined in. A wish spoken from a hospital bed became a scene repeated across America every summer.

---

## What One Cup of Lemonade Built

More than two decades after Alex's passing, the foundation bearing her name has raised **over $300 million** and funded more than **1,500 childhood cancer research projects**. Money collected less than a dollar at a time now fuels new drug development and clinical research happening somewhere, right now, at this very moment.

---

## Epilogue: A Stand That's Still Open

Alex Scott was born with a diagnosis that said she might never walk. She walked anyway — all the way to her front yard, where she set up a small table.

Her time in this world lasted only eight years. But the question she asked in those eight years — *"If I'm sick, can't I still help someone else?"* — is still being answered today, by small hands setting up stands in front yards all across America.

> *"When I get out, I want to have a lemonade stand."*

That single sentence from a hospital room has grown into more than $300 million for childhood cancer research — and into a new small stand being set up somewhere, even today.
