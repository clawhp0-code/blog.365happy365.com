## From the Streets of Uganda to America's Most Devoted Dad

### A Childhood of Abandonment

Peter Mutabazi was born into a poor family in Uganda. At just ten years old, he fled an abusive father and found himself living on the streets — sleeping under market stalls, scavenging for food, invisible to the world. Nobody came looking for him. Nobody wanted him.

Then, one day, an aid worker from an NGO noticed the boy. That single act of attention changed the trajectory of Peter's life. He was given access to education, and over the years, he built a career in international humanitarian work. Eventually, Peter immigrated to the United States and settled in Charlotte, North Carolina.

But no matter how far he traveled from those Ugandan streets, one question never left him: *What can I do for children like the boy I once was?*

---

### Anthony: The Boy Nobody Wanted

In 2018, Peter learned about America's foster care system — a network meant to protect children, but one that often leaves them feeling like luggage shuffled between temporary homes. More than 400,000 children are in foster care in the United States at any given time. Tens of thousands age out of the system without ever finding a permanent family.

Peter completed the training to become a foster parent and opened his home.

Soon after, he received a phone call from a social worker. Her voice carried the weight of urgency.

**"His name is Anthony. He's eleven years old. He's just been disrupted from his third foster placement. If we can't find someone by this weekend, he goes to a group home. Can you take him?"**

Three families had already said no to this child. Three times, Anthony had packed his belongings and been told he wasn't wanted. Peter knew that feeling with every fiber of his being. He didn't hesitate.

**"I'll be there."**

---

### A Trash Bag Full of Belongings

When Anthony arrived at Peter's home, he carried everything he owned in a black trash bag. Not a suitcase. Not a backpack. A garbage bag. It was standard practice in the system — a small, devastating detail that spoke volumes about how these children were regarded.

On the first night, Anthony looked at Peter and asked a question that broke his heart:

*"When are you going to get rid of me too?"*

Peter knelt down, looked the boy in the eyes, and said:

**"You're not going anywhere. As long as you want to be here, this is your home."**

Anthony didn't believe him. How could he? Trust had been shattered three times already. But Peter understood that trust isn't rebuilt with grand gestures — it's rebuilt through relentless, boring, beautiful consistency. He woke Anthony up every morning. Made breakfast. Drove him to school. Helped with homework. Tucked him in at night. He showed up, day after day, without fail.

Months passed. Then, one evening, as casually as if he'd been saying it his whole life, Anthony called Peter "Dad" for the first time.

Peter went to the kitchen and wept.

In 2020, Peter Mutabazi officially adopted Anthony.

---

### "No Child Is Unwanted"

Peter didn't stop with Anthony. He continued fostering other children and began sharing his journey on social media. His Instagram account (@petermutabazi) became a window into the everyday beauty of chosen family — cooking together, celebrating small victories, navigating the messy, glorious reality of fatherhood.

Millions of people around the world were moved by his story. News outlets from Good Morning America to People Magazine featured Peter and Anthony. In 2022, Peter published his memoir, **"Now I Am Known: How a Street Kid Turned Foster Dad Found Acceptance and True Worth."**

In the book, he wrote:

> *"Every child needs to be wanted by someone. I grew up unwanted. That's why I know — one person's 'yes' can change a child's entire life."*

Peter continues to foster children and travels across the United States speaking about the urgent need for foster and adoptive families. He doesn't claim to be a perfect parent. He simply aims to be the person who says what every child deserves to hear:

**"You don't have to go anywhere. You are home."**

---

### Why This Story Matters

What makes Peter Mutabazi's story extraordinary is its circularity. He was the abandoned child. He was the one nobody wanted. And instead of letting that wound define him with bitterness, he transformed it into a mission of radical love.

On that Friday afternoon when Anthony was rejected for the third time, the system had nearly given up on him. One phone call. One answer. One word — **"Yes."**

That's all it took to change everything.

Sometimes the world doesn't need a superhero. It just needs one person willing to show up. Peter Mutabazi showed up, and he kept showing up, every single day. In doing so, he proved something profoundly simple: **the most powerful force on earth is a person who refuses to let a child feel unwanted.**

As Peter often says in his speeches: *"I cannot change the whole world. But I can change the whole world for one child."*

Today, Anthony is a thriving teenager with a father who will never let go. And Peter — the boy who once slept under market stalls in Uganda — has become living proof that love is not bound by blood, borders, or the brokenness of our past.
